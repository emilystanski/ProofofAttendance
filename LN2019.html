2019: Lanyard

Year Role Age Team Div/Tier Final Rank Location
2019 Athlete 15 E381 Mike 15U D2 T2 49/118 Rim Park, Waterloo
Notes: At the beginning of September, before tryouts, my family and I went to Darien Lake for the weekend, and returned with my entire house flooded. I will never forget opening the door to my house, seeing the water everywhere, and then the ceiling above my dining room collapsing in front of me. I ended up having to get new gear for volleyball as my whole room had become water-damaged. Although I was feeling weird about my spot on the team, I had decided not to try out for other teams due to everything with my house and being promised a spot on the team at the end of the season. However, this "promise” was double-sided, as they promised I would make a team, but didn’t specify Black or Red. I ended up receiving an Impact Red offer and was devastated, as I knew the majority of the girls on that team were not on the same level of play. My mom and I had made the decision to decline and try out for whatever team was holding last-minute tryouts. Since my house was flooded, we lived in hotels for 4 months around Mississauga, which vastly opened up options for teams. I chose to go to E381, a Serbian club in Etobicoke, as the other club's tryout landed on my birthday, and I accepted an offer with E381. On this team, we had every level of play, which balanced out into Championship. This was also the year I had decided to prioritize my mental health and told my parents I did not want to pursue a post-secondary volleyball career, which was a difficult conversation that I still do not regret.
Although I already had an interesting year with this team, from parent coaches to teammates telling their parents to “f*** off”, 2019 provincials takes the cake. In 2019, the OVA created a rule that an athlete cannot participate in 2 provincials without a minimum week break in between the two events. My star teammate was playing on the 15U,16U,17U, and 18U teams, but 15U was her age and her priority. However, the owner of this club was, and still is, a grandfather figure to her, so she had complete trust in him, which turned out to be a mistake. My teammate had played in 18U provincials, which was directly before our event, and the club owner had manipulated her into thinking that he had spoken to the OVA and she would be allowed to participate in two provincials back to back. That 18U team ended up winning a medal, and every year the OVA posts medal winners from each provincials. Our second match of the day was against another Etobicoke-based team, and many of my teammates had friends or acquaintances on that team. After we had won, a mom from the opposing team, whose daughter was one of my teammate's best friends, went to the OVA and told them my teammate played in the provincials the day before. Because of this, our entire first day was disqualified; my teammate was not allowed to play, and the 18U team had their win disqualified and medals taken away. Turns out, the club owner had written a fake name, number, and age on his roster so his star player could play 18U because he was fully aware she was not allowed to play two events in a row. I remember sitting and watching another team play with my teammate, whose friend’s Mom had said something to the OVA. As we are watching, my teammate's mom approaches her and says, “Listen, sweetie, I know that this is your best friend, and I think she is super sweet and lovely. However, if you ever see her mom, please continue to be respectful, but never talk to that f***ing b**** ever again,” and walked away. I honestly think that set the tone for the rest of provincials and created a fire inside my team to win. Since we had been knocked down a tier, we had an easier pool the next day, which helped us secure a D2 T2 gold medal.
