2025: Merch

Year Role Age Team Div/Tier Final Rank Location
2025 Assistant Coach 21 Smash Raiders Rockets 15U D1 T3 30/267 EY Centre, Ottawa
Notes: In my 2019-2020 season, I played at a team in Richmond Hill called Reach, which was the best team environment I could’ve asked for. Before Provincials of that year, we would have gone in seeded 30th, which meant we would’ve been at the top of Division 2. However, COVID happened, and provincials did not happen in 2020 or 2021, and I had graduated high school in 2021, which would have been my last year able to play club volleyball. Due to bad coaches and negative teammates, I had completely blocked volleyball out of my mind. Truthfully, I wanted nothing to do with it as it had brought back many negative memories.
In the summer of 2024, I began having stomach issues that later on was diagnosed as a chronic illness. However, it took over a year to get that diagnosis as the Canadian healthcare system is backed up, but that’s a different story. That following October, I made the difficult decision to let go of working as it was contributing to worsening symptoms. After that, I was very depressed and had lost my sense of purpose. At the time, my sister had worked in the same establishment as I and had encountered a gentleman wearing a volleyball club sweater, so she decided to ask him about it. He turned out to be a coach for that club and encouraged my sister to start coaching, as he could tell she was very passionate about the sport. My sister decided to follow through with the opportunity, and truthfully, I got jealous and had a lot of new free time, and decided to join her. From there, the rest is history as I am finishing my second year coaching with my sister.
The 2024-25 season was my first year coaching, and I was placed with the 15U “C Team.” Although there is a stigma with being the 3rd 15U team, those girls worked their butts off to prove everyone wrong. With them ending up in primer, placing D1 in provincials, and being the most decorated team in the club that year, they exceeded everyone’s expectations, including my own. From the athletes to my amazing beach coach, my first year was one that's truly memorable. Being at provincials as an athlete compared to a coach is vastly different. For starters, I am an adult and have to do everything for myself. However, that comes with the perks of parent coffee runs, which I heavily relied on during those 3 days. I had driven my coaching staff to Ottawa, including driving us 30 minutes to the venue every morning at 6:30 am. I wish I could have enjoyed provincials more last year, but the morning wave prohibited that. I was half-asleep in the morning, would come back and sleep until dinner, go to dinner, shower, and repeat for 3 days. We did not do any exploring since we were so tired from such long mornings. Although we had lost 13-15 in the 3rd set in the quarter-final match, they won the entire year for me, and all of them hold a special place in my heart.
